Before 2026-07-18 this is a growth-only history, not a closures record. NPPES (the federal registry this is built on) does not publicly expose when a pharmacy deactivated its registration, so for earlier dates a pharmacy that has since closed still appears present at every date after it first registered, and one that closed and deactivated before July 2026 is missing entirely. Real observed snapshots begin 2026-07-18, and the registry has been re-swept daily since 2026-10-15: a location drops off once none of its registrations are active. An inactive registration isn't proof a pharmacy closed, and pharmacies often don't deactivate promptly when they do, so closures can lag or never appear. Newly registered pharmacies aren't added until they're reviewed.
Dates before ~2008 reflect a registration deadline, not necessarily real openings. Roughly half of today's pharmacy registrations (49%) date from 2006, and 80% of those from May–August 2006, lining up with the federal NPI system's rollout — many of those pharmacies had already existed for years and were simply registering for the first time because it became mandatory, not opening that week.
Desert status is geographic proximity, not travel access. A block group is a desert when it is low-income and its residents' population-weighted center is more than 1 mile (urban) or 10 miles (rural) in a straight line from the nearest confirmed walk-in pharmacy (Oregon/Idaho border pharmacies count). "Low income" uses a county median computed from Census data as a proxy for HUD's area median income, not HUD's published figure. Locations still in the REVIEW bucket (dashed amber outline) aren't counted yet, so a desert can contain one — some deserts may drop out once those are resolved.
