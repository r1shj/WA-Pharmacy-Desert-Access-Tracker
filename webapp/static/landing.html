Washington State

Pharmacy Access & Shortage Tracker

Where do Washingtonians live relative to a pharmacy, which communities are pharmacy deserts, and which of them may have the least room to adapt when national drug shortages hit?

Pharmacy Access Map Every pharmacy location in the state, and which neighborhoods are pharmacy deserts. Shortage Context Map FDA's national drug-shortage list, and a community vulnerability index.

How it works

Pharmacies

Pharmacy registrations come from the federal NPPES provider registry, filtered to locations physically in Washington, classified by type (walk-in retail, clinic, mail-order, and so on) and by ownership (chain, health system, independent), then geocoded with the U.S. Census Bureau geocoder. Several registrations at the same storefront are merged into one physical location.

Pharmacy deserts

Each of Washington's census block groups is measured from where its residents actually live (its population-weighted center) to the nearest walk-in pharmacy, including pharmacies just across the Oregon and Idaho borders. A block group is a desert when it is both low-income and farther than 1 mile (urban) or 10 miles (rural) from a pharmacy.

Shortage context

The FDA's national drug-shortage list is checked every day. A vulnerability index combines each block group's pharmacy access, income, and current national shortages in chronic-condition drug categories, to show which communities may have the least capacity to adapt when shortages occur.

Two timelines

Each map has its own date slider. On the pharmacy map, it shows which pharmacies had registered by a given date, back to 2005, and from October 2026 on, which were still active in the daily registry sweep. On the shortage map, it shows which national shortages FDA listed on a given date, back to 2012, and recomputes the vulnerability index for that day.

What this can't tell you

  • It doesn't know what's in stock. Shortages are reported nationally; no public source covers what any specific pharmacy has on its shelves. Check with your local pharmacy for availability.
  • A registered pharmacy isn't confirmed open. The federal registry doesn't reliably record closures.
  • Distances are straight-line, not driving distance — real trips across water or mountains are longer.
  • The low-income threshold is an approximation of HUD's 80%-of-area-median standard, computed from Census data rather than HUD's own published figures.

Every known gap is documented in LIMITATIONS.md, and every methodological choice in DECISIONS.md.
