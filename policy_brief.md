# Pharmacy Deserts in Washington State

*Policy brief, data as of 2026-10-16.* Source: [wa-pharmacy-desert-access-tracker.onrender.com](https://wa-pharmacy-desert-access-tracker.onrender.com)

**Bottom line**

- **237 of Washington's 5,311 census block groups (346,334 residents) are pharmacy deserts**: low-income *and* more than 1 mile (urban) or 10 miles (rural) from a pharmacy. Counting 447 pharmacy locations whose walk-in status is still unconfirmed would lower that to **176 block groups (about 255,000 residents)**. The true figure lies in that range.
- **Most desert residents live in metro counties.** King, Pierce, Clark and Snohomish hold 64% of them. The highest *shares* are in small rural counties, and the longest distances are in rural Lewis, Pend Oreille and Stevens counties (19-33 straight-line miles).
- **The vulnerability index adds no geographic information beyond access and income.** Its national shortage component is one statewide number.

**How "desert" is measured.** Distance runs from each block group's 2020 Census population-weighted center to the nearest confirmed walk-in pharmacy, counting Oregon and Idaho pharmacies within 20 miles of the border. It is straight-line distance, meaning geographic proximity, not travel time. "Low income" means a tract's median household income is below 80% of a county median we computed from ACS data. That's a proxy for HUD's area median income, not HUD's published figure.

**Highest share of county residents in deserts:** Skamania 21.5% (2 block groups, both review-sensitive), Pend Oreille 15.5%, Whitman 13.6%, Stevens 11.5%, Kittitas 10.7%, Grant 10.1%. Ten of the 39 counties have no desert block groups.

\*Review-sensitive: within the distance threshold of a pharmacy whose walk-in status is still unconfirmed. These would stop being deserts if those pharmacies are confirmed.

**Notable block groups.**

- **Farthest from a pharmacy:** all rural, and none review-sensitive. Four are in Lewis County, 28-33 miles away (530419720001-004). Three are in Pend Oreille County, 26.5-32 miles away (530519701001-003). Four are in Stevens County, 19-25 miles away.
- **Most populous deserts:** urban Pierce County block groups 530530729032, 530530729081 and 530530729011 (4,374, 4,048 and 3,195 residents), 1.7-3.5 miles from a pharmacy.

**Vulnerability index.**

- **How it's built:** 40 points for low access, 40 for low income, and up to 20 for national shortages. Today 103 shortages are active in 7 chronic-condition categories, worth 14 points, the same in every block group.
- **What it shows:** its "high" band is exactly the 237 deserts. The medium band (2,498 block groups) is low-access-only (1,212) or low-income-only (1,286). County rankings by vulnerability therefore match the desert rankings above.
- **What it doesn't show:** whether any medicine is unavailable in Washington. FDA shortages are national, and no public source reports pharmacy-level stock.

**Pharmacy growth (growth only).**

- **The pre-2008 artifact:** of the 1,624 mapped locations active today, 1,060 (65%) first registered before 2008. That reflects the NPI registration deadline, not real openings.
- **Since then:** 15-48 of today's locations first registered in each full year from 2008 to 2025. That was 38-48 a year in 2013-2016 and 15-24 a year in 2020-2023.
- **What it can't show:** closures. So it shows when today's pharmacies first registered, not net change in access.

**What this data cannot support.**

- It can't support any claim of observed local drug shortages.
- It can't show that any pharmacy is open or licensed: NPPES registration only, not validated against WA Department of Health licensing.
- It doesn't support conclusions about which ownership types serve deserts. 392 pharmacies have unknown ownership.

**Next steps that would sharpen these findings:**

1. Confirm walk-in status for the 474 review records, which would collapse the 176-237 range.
2. Validate against WA DOH facility licenses.
3. Use road-network travel times for the rural block groups above.
